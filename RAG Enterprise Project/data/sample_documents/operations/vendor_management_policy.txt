INTRODUCTION

This policy defines how Northstar Systems, Inc. identifies, evaluates, approves, monitors, and manages external vendors and service providers.

The policy applies to all departments purchasing goods or services from external organizations.

The objective is to ensure that vendors meet Northstar's financial, operational, security, legal, and service-quality requirements.

Vendor management activities must comply with the FY2026 Annual Operating Budget FIN-BUD-001 and Procurement Guidelines OPS-POL-002.

1. VENDOR CLASSIFICATION

Vendors are classified into three categories.

Category A - Critical Vendors:

Vendors providing services essential to business continuity.

Examples include:
- Cloud infrastructure providers.
- Production database providers.
- Security monitoring providers.
- Critical enterprise software platforms.

Category B - Operational Vendors:

Vendors supporting regular department operations.

Examples include:
- Office facility providers.
- Equipment suppliers.
- Logistics companies.
- Professional consulting services.

Category C - Low-Risk Vendors:

Vendors providing low-value, non-critical products or services.

Examples include:
- Office stationery suppliers.
- General printing services.
- Small administrative service providers.

The vendor classification determines the depth of due diligence and monitoring required.

2. VENDOR ONBOARDING PROCESS

All new vendors must complete the official vendor onboarding process before receiving a purchase order or contract.

The process consists of six stages.

Stage 1: Vendor Request

The requesting department submits a vendor registration request to Operations.

The request must include:
- Vendor legal name.
- Business address.
- Contact details.
- Description of products or services.
- Estimated annual contract value.
- Requesting department.
- Business justification.

Stage 2: Initial Evaluation

Operations verifies the vendor's business identity and evaluates whether the proposed service satisfies the department's requirements.

The requesting department must explain why the vendor is needed.

Stage 3: Financial and Legal Review

Finance verifies pricing, budget availability, payment terms, and financial approval requirements.

Legal reviews contracts, liability clauses, confidentiality obligations, and termination conditions where applicable.

Stage 4: Security Assessment

Vendors handling company systems, customer data, or confidential information must complete a security assessment.

Security Operations reviews:
- Data protection practices.
- Encryption requirements.
- Access controls.
- Incident reporting procedures.
- Business continuity arrangements.

Critical vendors must provide appropriate security documentation before approval.

Stage 5: Final Approval

Operations records the assessment results and submits the vendor for final approval.

The applicable department and financial approval authorities must approve the commitment before contract signature.

Stage 6: Vendor Registration

Approved vendors are entered into the central vendor register.

Each vendor receives a unique vendor identifier.

The vendor register stores:
- Vendor ID.
- Legal name.
- Vendor category.
- Approval date.
- Contract expiry date.
- Department owner.
- Risk classification.
- Current approval status.

3. FINANCIAL APPROVAL REQUIREMENTS

Vendor commitments follow the company approval matrix.

Up to $2,500:
Department Manager approval.

Above $2,500 and up to $10,000:
Department Director approval.

Above $10,000:
Finance Director approval.

Above $50,000:
Finance Director and CFO approval.

These requirements apply to the entire expected commitment, including recurring fees and applicable taxes.

New recurring engineering infrastructure commitments exceeding $25,000 annually additionally require approval from the VP of Engineering and Finance Director.

Vendor approval does not authorize spending beyond the department's approved budget.

4. VENDOR RISK ASSESSMENT

Operations and Security assign a risk classification to each vendor.

Critical vendors require detailed due diligence before onboarding.

The assessment evaluates:
- Financial stability.
- Operational capability.
- Information security.
- Regulatory compliance.
- Business continuity.
- Service reliability.
- Data handling practices.

Vendors processing customer information must agree to appropriate confidentiality and data-protection requirements.

5. CONTRACT MANAGEMENT

All vendor contracts must be stored in the approved contract repository.

Contracts must identify:
- Scope of services.
- Contract duration.
- Pricing and payment terms.
- Service-level agreements.
- Confidentiality requirements.
- Data protection obligations.
- Termination conditions.
- Renewal provisions.

Department owners must monitor contract expiry dates.

Renewal requests should be initiated at least 60 calendar days before contract expiration.

6. VENDOR PERFORMANCE MONITORING

Critical vendors are reviewed quarterly.

Operational vendors are reviewed at least twice annually.

Low-risk vendors are reviewed annually or when significant issues arise.

Performance evaluations consider:
- Delivery reliability.
- Product or service quality.
- Contract compliance.
- Support responsiveness.
- Pricing consistency.
- Security incidents.
- Service-level agreement performance.

Vendors failing to meet contractual requirements may be placed on a corrective action plan.

7. VENDOR SUSPENSION AND TERMINATION

A vendor may be suspended when:
- Serious security vulnerabilities are identified.
- Contractual obligations are repeatedly violated.
- Fraud or unethical conduct is suspected.
- Required regulatory documentation is missing.
- Business continuity risks become unacceptable.

Operations coordinates suspension decisions with Legal, Finance, and Security.

Termination must follow contractual conditions.

Outstanding invoices and company assets must be reconciled before closure.

8. RESPONSIBILITIES

Operations owns vendor registration and ongoing vendor records.

Requesting departments define business requirements and monitor service delivery.

Finance validates budgets and financial approvals.

Legal reviews contractual obligations.

Security Operations evaluates information security risks.

Department Directors approve business requirements within their authority.

The CFO approves commitments exceeding $50,000.

9. RECORD RETENTION

Vendor registration records, due diligence documents, contracts, and performance reviews must be retained for seven years after contract termination, or longer where local law requires.
