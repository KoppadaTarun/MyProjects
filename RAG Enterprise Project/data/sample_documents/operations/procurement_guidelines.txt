INTRODUCTION

These guidelines define the procedures for purchasing goods, equipment, software, and services at Northstar Systems, Inc.

The procurement process ensures that company purchases are commercially reasonable, properly approved, within approved budgets, and compliant with internal controls.

All procurement activities must follow the Vendor Management Policy OPS-POL-001 and the FY2026 Annual Operating Budget FIN-BUD-001.

1. PROCUREMENT PRINCIPLES

Every purchase must satisfy the following requirements:

Business Necessity:
The requesting department must explain why the product or service is required.

Budget Availability:
The department must confirm that sufficient approved budget is available.

Competitive Pricing:
Purchases should use competitive quotations where practical.

Vendor Approval:
The selected supplier must satisfy applicable vendor onboarding requirements.

Authorization:
The purchase must receive approval from the correct financial authority.

Documentation:
All purchase decisions and supporting records must be maintained.

Employees must not divide purchases into multiple smaller transactions to avoid financial approval requirements.

2. PURCHASE REQUEST PROCESS

All purchases begin with a Purchase Requisition (PR).

The requesting employee submits the PR through the procurement portal.

The request must contain:

Purchase Requisition ID.

Requesting department.

Requested product or service.

Business justification.

Estimated total cost.

Preferred vendor, if known.

Required delivery date.

Budget cost center.

Supporting quotations.

The department manager reviews the request for business necessity and budget availability.

Operations checks procurement compliance and vendor eligibility.

Finance validates financial authorization requirements.

Once approved, the procurement team issues a Purchase Order (PO).

Employees must not instruct vendors to begin work before the required approval and purchase order are completed, except where an authorized emergency exception applies.

3. FINANCIAL APPROVAL MATRIX

Northstar uses the following procurement approval thresholds.

Purchase value up to $2,500:

Department Manager approval.

Purchase value above $2,500 and up to $10,000:

Department Director approval.

Purchase value above $10,000:

Finance Director approval before commitment.

Purchase value above $50,000:

Finance Director and CFO approval.

The thresholds apply to the total expected commitment rather than individual invoice amounts.

Recurring contracts must be evaluated using their full known contractual commitment.

For example, a service costing $4,000 per month under a 12-month contract represents a $48,000 commitment.

That contract requires Finance Director approval because its total value exceeds $10,000.

It also requires the relevant department and Operations reviews.

4. COMPETITIVE QUOTATION REQUIREMENTS

The following quotation requirements apply:

Purchases up to $2,500:
One quotation or documented market-price justification.

Purchases above $2,500 and up to $10,000:
At least two competitive quotations where practical.

Purchases above $10,000 and up to $50,000:
At least three competitive quotations.

Purchases above $50,000:
At least three competitive quotations, a documented commercial evaluation, and CFO approval.

When fewer quotations are available, the requesting department must provide a written justification.

Single-source procurement is permitted only when there is a legitimate business reason, such as a proprietary product, compatibility requirement, or emergency.

Single-source exceptions require documented approval from the Operations Director and the applicable financial authority.

5. PURCHASE ORDER AND CONTRACT REQUIREMENTS

An approved Purchase Requisition is converted into a Purchase Order.

The Purchase Order must contain:
- PO identifier.
- Vendor identifier.
- Description of goods or services.
- Quantity.
- Agreed pricing.
- Delivery terms.
- Payment terms.
- Requesting department.
- Required approvals.

Contracts must be reviewed according to the Vendor Management Policy.

Legal review is required for agreements containing significant liability, confidentiality, intellectual property, or data-protection obligations.

6. SOFTWARE AND TECHNOLOGY PROCUREMENT

All software and cloud-service purchases require additional technical and security review.

The requesting department must identify:
- Business purpose.
- Data being processed.
- Required user access.
- Integration requirements.
- Subscription duration.
- Vendor security documentation.

Security Operations must review software that processes confidential company information or customer data.

Engineering must evaluate software that connects to production infrastructure.

Unapproved software must not be installed on company devices or connected to production systems.

7. GOODS RECEIPT AND INVOICE VERIFICATION

After delivery, the requesting department confirms that goods or services satisfy the Purchase Order.

The receiving employee records a Goods Receipt Note (GRN) where applicable.

Finance performs a three-way match between:

Purchase Order:
Confirms the authorized purchase.

Goods Receipt Note:
Confirms that goods or services were delivered.

Vendor Invoice:
Confirms the amount being requested for payment.

Invoices containing discrepancies must be placed on hold until the issue is resolved.

Partial deliveries must be documented before partial payment is processed.

8. EMERGENCY PROCUREMENT

Emergency purchases may be necessary to restore critical operations or address immediate security and safety concerns.

The requesting department must document:
- Nature of the emergency.
- Reason normal procurement could not be followed.
- Estimated expenditure.
- Vendor selection justification.
- Required approvals.

Emergency procurement does not remove financial approval requirements.

The department must submit supporting documentation to Operations within two business days after the purchase.

9. ASSET MANAGEMENT

Equipment purchased for company use must be registered in the company asset register where applicable.

The asset register contains:
- Asset ID.
- Purchase Order number.
- Purchase date.
- Purchase cost.
- Assigned employee or department.
- Physical location.
- Warranty information.
- Current asset status.

Employees are responsible for protecting assigned equipment.

Disposal or transfer of company assets requires Operations approval.

10. PROCUREMENT RESPONSIBILITIES

Requesting departments identify business needs and prepare purchase requests.

Department Managers verify business necessity and budget availability.

Department Directors authorize purchases within their approval authority.

Operations manages supplier selection, purchase orders, and procurement records.

Finance validates budget availability, financial approvals, and invoice payments.

Security Operations reviews technology and data-related risks.

Legal reviews contractual obligations.

The CFO approves commitments exceeding $50,000.

11. RECORD RETENTION AND AUDIT

Purchase requisitions, quotations, approvals, purchase orders, invoices, and goods receipt records must be retained for seven years or longer where local law requires.

Finance and Internal Audit may review procurement transactions to verify compliance.

Violations may result in purchase cancellation, repayment obligations, or disciplinary action.
